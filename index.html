Dimmer math

The new LED bulbs flicker on the old dimmer, the electrician shrugs, and nobody can say what dimming actually saves. Dimmer math totals your bulb wattage against the dimmer's minimum and maximum load so the flicker gets a cause, counts how many bulbs fit the window, and prices a year of dimmed evenings in kilowatt-hours and dollars. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Load plan - bulbs and wattage against the dimmer's window: in range, under the minimum, or over the maximum.
  • Savings plan - a dim level becomes watts saved, kilowatt-hours a year, and money.
  • Bulb-count plan - how many bulbs of a given wattage fit between the dimmer's minimum and maximum.

Labeled norms

Labeled approximation: LED draw tracks the dim level roughly linearly; real drivers vary. Labeled reference: many LED dimmers want a 5 W to 150 W load, older leading-edge dimmers often a 25 W minimum, and LED-rated trailing-edge dimmers flicker less. These are illustrative estimates - the dimmer's datasheet and a licensed electrician's advice win, and dimmers on circuits you cannot identify are an electrician's job.
